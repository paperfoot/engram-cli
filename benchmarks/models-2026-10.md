# Model refresh, October 2026

Measured 29 Oct 2026. Scripts: `benchmarks/models_extraction_eval.py`, `benchmarks/jev/scifact_grounding_probe.py`.

## Fact extraction (`remember`)

Set: the September remember set, 60 seeded LoCoMo windows plus the 20 health notes in
`jev/remember-health-notes.jsonl` (80 memories). The call is remember's own, verbatim:
`FACT_EXTRACTION_PROMPT` + date context, temperature 0, `max_tokens` 2048.

- **JEV unsupported**: every extracted fact through the shipped grounding question
  (`judge_eval grounding`, `jev-1.13.0`); p < 0.9 counts as unsupported. JEV at 0.9 also
  rejects some true facts, so read the column as a ranking, not an error rate.
- **Sonnet not_supported**: a second, independent checker (`anthropic/claude-sonnet-5.5`,
  the September labelling rubric) on the three leading models.
- **Safety recall**: 28 gold facts from the health notes (current and stopped medications,
  allergies and non-allergies, diagnoses ruled in or out), each checked against that note's
  extracted facts with one JEV question; p ≥ 0.5 counts as recalled.
- **$/1k**: OpenRouter's billed cost per 1,000 memories. **p50/p95**: wall time per call.
- `@effort` sends OpenRouter `reasoning.effort`.

Update, 29 Oct: the first pass missed the newer releases, and its comparison of them was
unfair: terra ran at `low` effort, DeepSeek at its default, and all 14 DeepSeek "failures" were
my 2,048-token cap cutting off its reasoning. Its "safety recall" also asked JEV whether a gold
fact's meaning appeared anywhere, not whether engram would file it under a safety relation, and
it never counted false safety records. Those two rows above are superseded.

Fair re-run (`models_extraction_fair.py`): both models get remember's exact request, effort
`low`; scored on engram's own canonical relations (parsed from `relations.rs`). Safety key: 17
facts that must be filed under `takes_medication` / `allergic_to` / `diagnosed_with` with the
right state, and 13 that must NOT exist as ongoing (non-allergies, stopped drugs, ruled-out
diagnoses, hedges, another person's drug). All 160 calls were served by the requested model.

| model | safety facts filed correctly | false safety records | unusable outputs | p50 s | p95 s | $/1k memories | providers |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `openai/gpt-6.1-terra@low` (**default**) | 16/17 | 0 | 0/80 | 4.14 | 6.22 | 1.411 | OpenAI |
| `deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash-1017@low` | 16/17 | 0 | 16/80 (cut off at 2,048 tokens of reasoning) | 4.73 | 15.48 | 0.734 | 15 third-party hosts |

Both miss the same fact (a thyroid nodule filed as `has_thyroid_nodule`, not `diagnosed_with`).
Safety is a tie. Terra is the default for reliability: DeepSeek ignores `low` effort on one
memory in five and returns nothing usable, its tail latency is 2.5x, and OpenRouter spreads it
over 15 hosts, each of which would see the memory. Limits: synthetic set (60 LoCoMo windows, 20
hand-written health notes), one run, small safety key; hallucination was not re-judged.

First-pass decision: `openai/gpt-5.6-luna` at `low` effort. It has the lowest unsupported rate under
both checkers (luna's default effort: 6.9% vs 18.5% by Sonnet), extracts 2.8x as many facts,
recalls one more safety fact and costs 10% less per memory. The price is latency: `remember`
waits for extraction, and p50 goes from 0.9 s to 4.7 s. Against luna's default effort, low
cuts latency 45% and cost 37%, and JEV scores it the same. The effort is sent only with the
default model; an `ENGRAM_FACT_MODEL` or `compiler.extraction_model` override gets the
provider's default, as before. Haiku 5.5 is the runner-up: best safety recall and cheaper, but
more unsupported facts than luna under both checkers (Sonnet 12.7% vs 6.9%). Without thinking
it falls below flash-lite.
Gemini 3.8 Flash is 8x the cost and no better. DeepSeek and Qwen break the JSON format.

One run per model, 80 memories: differences under about 5 points in the unsupported columns
are noise.

## JEV version

`jev-1.14.0` shipped 14 Oct (`jev-latest` now points to it). Same API, a drop-in. SciFact
probe, all 1,189 human-labelled pairs:

| question | jev-1.13.0 AUC | jev-1.14.0 AUC | p50 / p95 s, 1.13 | p50 / p95 s, 1.14 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| plain | 0.9650 | 0.9640 | 0.20 / 0.37 | 0.22 / 1.18 |
| strict | 0.9625 | 0.9622 | 0.21 / 0.37 | 0.27 / 1.21 |

1.14 does not reach 1.13's AUC (the bar), so the pin stays at `jev-1.13.0`.
`typesafe/jev-router` on OpenRouter is not a judge: it is a chat-model router that uses Jev to
pick a model and effort per request.

## Other defaults

- Ad-hoc chat default and the bench answerer: `anthropic/claude-opus-5` → `anthropic/claude-opus-5.5`
  (cheaper: $4/$20 vs $5/$25 per M tokens). The bench grader stays on `gemini-3.1-pro-preview`
  so recorded runs remain comparable.
- Synthesis (`compile --llm` only): `google/gemini-3.6-flash` → `google/gemini-3.8-flash`, the current
  Gemini flash ($0.825/$4.125 vs $0.75/$3.75). Not evaluated; the path is slated for deletion.
  No Gemini 4 flash-class model exists yet; `google/gemini-4-argon` is a $2/$10 flagship.

## Embeddings and rerank (unchanged)

`gemini-embedding-2` is still Google's newest embedding model (the API lists only `-001`,
`-2-preview` and `-2`). Cohere rerank was not re-checked (no Cohere key on this machine).
Changing either means re-embedding or re-scoring the store, which is a separate decision.
